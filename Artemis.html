Artemis II: Flight Day 6 Guide (Moon Flyby)

Time (EDT / GMT) Event What to Know
9:50 a.m. / 1350 Crew Wake-Up Beginning of Flight Day 6 (Flyby Day). NASA will play a wake-up song for the crew.
1:00 p.m. / 1700 NASA Livestream Begins Special broadcast coverage starts; the 24/7 feed transitions to this event.
1:56 p.m. / 1756 Distance Record Broken Orion surpasses the Apollo 13 record, traveling beyond 248,655 miles from Earth.
2:10 p.m. / 1810 Crew Commentary The crew will provide audio-only comments on reaching the record-breaking distance.
2:15 p.m. / 1815 Cabin Configuration Astronauts darken cabin lights and prepare cameras and gear for the flyby.
2:45 p.m. / 1845 Observation Period Begins Seven-hour moon flyby observation starts; the main phase lasts five hours.
4:35 p.m. / 2035 Interior Views NASA will broadcast live interior views of the Orion spacecraft during the flyby.
6:47 p.m. / 2247 Loss of Signal NASA will temporarily lose contact with Artemis II for approximately 40 minutes.
7:02 p.m. / 2302 Closest Lunar Approach Orion will reach its nearest point to the moon, at an altitude of about 4,070 miles.
7:05 p.m. / 2305 Maximum Distance from Earth Orion reaches its farthest point from Earth, approximately 252,760 miles away.
7:27 p.m. / 2327 Reacquisition of Signal Mission Control is expected to restore communication with the spacecraft.
8:35 p.m. / 0035* Solar Eclipse Astronauts witness a total solar eclipse as the moon blocks the sun for 53 minutes.
9:20 p.m. / 0120* Observation Period Ends Flyby observing period concludes; crew begins transmitting imagery to Earth.

*GMT times occur on April 7.
